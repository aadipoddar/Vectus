🤔1 · What is "payroll", really?

Payroll is just this: at the end of every month, work out how much money each worker takes home, and make sure the government gets the bits the law says it must.

🏭 Companyhas work to be done
→
👷 Employeedoes the work for a month
→
💰 Salarycompany owes them money

But the worker doesn't get all of that money in hand. A few small pieces are taken out first — some is the worker's own savings, some is tax for the government. What's left is what actually lands in their bank account.

🍕 Think of a pizza. The whole pizza is the salary the company promises. Before it reaches the worker, a couple of slices are set aside: one slice goes into the worker's own piggy bank (savings), one or two slices go to the government (tax). The remaining slices are what the worker eats today. Payroll's job is to cut the pizza correctly, every month, for every worker — and never make a mistake, because it's people's money.

✅ Why does software do this instead of a person with a calculator? Because a factory can have hundreds of workers, the cuts follow fussy government rules that change, and a single wrong rupee can mean a fine. Software does it the same correct way every time, in seconds.

📖2 · The 8 words you'll hear every single day

Learn these and you'll understand 90% of conversations. Each has a simple meaning.

WordIn plain EnglishEveryday comparison
EarningMoney added to the worker's pay.Things that grow your pizza.
DeductionMoney taken out of the pay.Slices removed from your pizza.
BasicThe core part of salary. Most other things are calculated from it.The plain base of the pizza.
HRA"House Rent Allowance" — extra pay to help with rent. Usually a % of Basic.A topping added on.
DA"Dearness Allowance" — extra pay to cope with rising prices. Also a % of Basic.Another topping.
GrossAll earnings added together, before any cuts.The whole pizza.
Net (Take-home)What's left after all deductions. The amount that hits the bank.The slices you actually eat.
CTC"Cost To Company" — the full yearly cost of an employee to the company, including the company's own contributions.What the pizza costs the shop, not just you.

🧮 The one formula that rules everything: Gross (all earnings) − Deductions (all cuts) = Net (take-home). That's it. Everything else is just how we work out each earning and each cut.

🏛️4 · PF, ESI, PT, TDS — the four "government cuts"

India's law says certain amounts must be cut from salary. There are four big ones. Here's each in one friendly line. You do NOT need to memorise the rates — the software stores them.

Short nameFull nameWhat it really isWho keeps itRough amount
PFProvident FundForced savings for the worker's retirement. The company adds a matching amount too.The worker (later)12% of Basic+DA
ESIEmployees' State InsuranceCheap health insurance for lower-paid workers. Only if salary ≤ ₹21,000.Govt health scheme0.75% of gross
PTProfessional TaxA small state tax for earning a living. A few rupees, fixed by salary band.State govt (West Bengal)₹0–₹200
TDSTax Deducted at SourceIncome tax collected a little each month instead of one big bill.Central govtDepends on yearly pay
🐷 PF = piggy bank the law makes you keep. 🏥 ESI = a cheap clinic card for lower earners. 🏙️ PT = a small "you earn here" fee to the state. 🧾 TDS = paying your yearly income tax in 12 small monthly bites.
🔑 The clever part of our design: these rates (12%, ₹21,000, etc.) are not written into the program. They're saved as settings in the database. So when the government changes a rate next year, someone just edits a setting — no programmer needed. Remember this idea; it's the heart of why the system is "professional."

🏗️5 · How our software is built — 3 simple layers

Every app like ours is a stack of three layers. Picture a restaurant:

🖥️ Layer 1 — The Screens (what you see & click)
The dining area & menu. Buttons, forms, tables, the payslip on screen.
⬇ talks to ⬇
🧠 Layer 2 — The Brain / Logic (the rules & the calculator)
The kitchen & chef. Takes your order, applies the recipes (PF, tax…), works out the numbers.
⬇ stores in ⬇
🗄️ Layer 3 — The Database / Memory (where everything is kept)
The pantry & record book. Remembers every employee, every month, forever.

What each layer is, in baby words

You'll hear…It simply means…Restaurant comparison
Page / Screen / UIA window you look at and click.The menu & table.
ComponentA small reusable screen-piece (a button, a text box, a dropdown). We build pages by snapping these together.Standard plates & cutlery reused at every table.
Table (database)A grid/spreadsheet the computer keeps, e.g. one table holds all employees, one row per employee.A page in the record book.
Stored procedureA saved instruction in the database that reads or writes those tables. We always go through these (never raw commands) — safer & consistent.A fixed recipe card the pantry-keeper follows.
EngineThe calculator brain that turns "Ramesh, 30 days worked" into "Net ₹27,650". Pure maths, no screens.The chef who cooks by the recipes.
ModelA simple description of one thing's shape — e.g. an Employee has a Name, a Code, a Bank A/c. Matches one database table.The blank order form's fields.
✅ Why three layers and not one big blob? If the tax screen looks ugly, you fix Layer 1 without touching the maths. If a tax rule changes, you fix Layer 2 without touching the screens. Each layer has one job, so changes are safe and easy. This is the single most important idea in how professional apps are built.

Two habits we follow everywhere (and why they matter)

🗑️ "Soft delete"When you "delete" something, we don't really erase it — we just tick a hidden "inactive" box. So nothing is ever truly lost, and you can bring it back. Money records must never vanish.
📝 "Audit trail"Every time someone adds or changes anything, we secretly write down who did it, when, and what changed. So if a number looks wrong, we can see its full history.

🌳7 · The building blocks (a tree)

Our payroll module is split into four groups. Think of it as a tree: the trunk is "Payroll", and it has four branches.

🧾 PAYROLL MODULE

🗂️ 1. Masters (set up once)

  • Employee — the people
  • Department — e.g. Production
  • Designation — e.g. Fitter
  • Location — e.g. Durgapur
  • Salary Components — Basic, HRA…
  • Salary Structure — each person's pay recipe

⚙️ 2. Rules (the law settings)

  • Statutory Rules — PF %, ESI %, tax bands
  • Stored as data, so they're easy to change

🔄 3. Processing (every month)

  • Attendance — days worked
  • Payroll Run — do the month for everyone
  • Advances — loans & their repayment
  • Arrears — back-pay for late raises

📊 4. Reports (the outputs)

  • Payslip — for each worker
  • Salary register — everyone, one sheet
  • Bank file — to pay salaries
  • Government returns — PF/ESI/PT/tax
🗂️ "Master" = a list you make once and reuse forever, like the Contacts in your phone. You add "Production" as a department once, then every employee just points to it. Set up the masters first; everything else leans on them.

8

Press "Process" — the brain calculates

This is where the engine (the calculator) does its work. It goes step by step. Watch it think:

1️⃣ Add earnings → GROSS = 20,000 + 8,000 + 3,000 + 1,600 = ₹32,600
⬇
2️⃣ PF = 12% of Basic+DA. Law caps the base at 15,000 → 12% × 15,000 = ₹1,800
⬇
3️⃣ ESI? His gross 32,600 is above ₹21,000 → not applicable (ESI is only for lower earners)
⬇
4️⃣ Professional Tax for his salary band in West Bengal = ₹150
⬇
5️⃣ Income Tax (TDS) this month = ₹2,000
⬇
6️⃣ Loan repayment (he borrowed ₹5,000, pays ₹1,000/month) = ₹1,000
⬇
7️⃣ NET = 32,600 − (1,800 + 150 + 2,000 + 1,000) = ₹27,650 🎉
✅ The ₹15,000 PF cap in plain words: the government says "we'll only count up to ₹15,000 of basic-type pay for PF, even if you earn more." Ramesh's Basic+DA is ₹23,000, but we use ₹15,000. 12% of that = ₹1,800. You don't have to remember this — the engine does.

10

Press "Finalize" — the accounting entry is written

Finalizing locks the month (so numbers can't change) and writes one note in the company's money diary (the "accounting journal"). In plain words it records: "We owe salary expense; part is held for the govt, part goes to Ramesh's bank."

The company's note says…Money in (Dr)Money owed (Cr)
Salary expense (the cost of work done)32,600
Held to send to PF office1,800
Held to send to govt (PT + Income tax)2,150
Loan he repaid (back to company)1,000
To pay into Ramesh's bank27,650
Both sides match ✓32,60032,600
⚖️ "Both sides match" is the golden rule of accounting (called double-entry). Every rupee that goes out must be explained by where it went. If the two columns aren't equal, something is wrong. Our app always makes them equal.
11

Generate the government report (PF "ECR")

Finally, the government's PF office wants a file each month listing how much PF for each worker. The app makes a plain text file (the "ECR") that you upload to the PF website. For Ramesh, one line:

ECR_April2026.txt
100200300400#~#RAMESH KUMAR#~#32600#~#15000#~#15000#~#15000#~#1800#~#1250#~#550#~#0#~#0

The funny #~# just separates the values (the PF website demands that exact format). It lists his PF number, name, wages, and the PF amounts. The app builds this for you — you never type it.

🎉 You did it! From an empty screen you set up the lists, added a person, ran their pay, printed a payslip, recorded the accounting, and produced a legal report. That's one full payroll cycle. For 200 employees it's the same steps — the app just repeats the maths for each.

What to do on your first real task

  1. Don't panic — set up Masters first (department, designation, location, components).
  2. Add an employee, then their salary structure.

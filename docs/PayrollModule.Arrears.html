1What arrears are

An arrear is back-pay: a salary revision takes effect from a past date, but is processed later — so the already-paid months were short, and the shortfall is paid in the current run.

Increment effective 1-Apr→ Apr & May run at old rate→ Revision approved in Jun→ Engine computes Apr+May shortfall→ Paid as arrears in Jun

Common triggers: delayed annual increment, promotion with retrospective effect, DA revision, wage-settlement, or a correction. Arrears can also be negative (a retrospective cut → recovery).

2The method — recompute each past period, take the difference

The arrear engine is a thin wrapper over the normal calculation engine. It never guesses a lump sum; it re-runs each affected month.

#StepWhat happens
1Find the windowAll finalized runs from the new EffectiveFrom up to the current open run.
2Recompute each monthRun Compute for that month under the new structure, with the same attendance/LOP actually used.
3DifferenceNew "should-have-been" earnings − what was actually paid (from stored PayrollLineDetail) = per-component arrear.
4Statutory per origin monthApply PF / ESI / PT to each month's difference using that month's ceilings & rates — the StatutoryRate line effective for that origin period — then sum. (Never on the lump sum — that would break the ₹15,000 / ₹21,000 ceilings.)
5Pay in current runTotal arrear earnings + arrear PF/ESI flow into the open run, tagged separately for Form 16.
Why per-period and not on the total? Statutory ceilings are monthly. Computing PF on a 2-month lump of ₹4,600 vs ₹2,300 × 2 only differs once a ceiling is crossed — but when it does (e.g. PF wage near ₹15,000), the lump-sum method is simply wrong. Per-period is always correct.

PF and ESI are payable on arrears, computed per origin month (both months below the ceilings here, so the full difference attracts contribution).

HeadRule for arrears
PT Professional TaxSlab is on the total gross paid in the paying month (June), arrears included — so arrears can push PT into a higher slab. Not computed per origin month.
TDSArrears are taxable in the year received (added to the June projection). Tagged separately so they appear on Form 16 Part B and a §89(1) working — see §6.
The mistake to avoid: skipping PF/ESI on arrears. EPFO/ESIC treat arrears as wages of the month paid; missing them invites interest (12% p.a.) and damages. The engine computes them automatically the moment a back-dated structure is saved.

Rounding is per origin period (PF nearest rupee, ESI rounded up) — so the per-month figures sum to the totals shown. Admin charges (A/c 2) are establishment-level and added on the challan, not per employee.

6Tax relief — Section 89(1) & Form 10E

Arrears can push an employee into a higher tax bracket in the receipt year. The law lets them claim relief so they aren't over-taxed for a timing accident.

StepSection 89(1) relief calculation (employee files Form 10E)
1–3Tax on receipt-year income with arrears, minus tax without arrears = extra tax now.
4–6Tax on each origin year with its arrears, minus without = tax that should have applied then.
=Relief = (extra tax now) − (tax that should have applied then).
Our responsibility is the data, not the relief. Payroll tags arrears separately so they print on Form 16 Part B and on a §89(1)/Form 10E working sheet. The employee (or their filing) claims the relief and must file Form 10E before the ITR, else the relief is disallowed. We never compute the relief into TDS unless the employer chooses to consider it.

7Data model & edge cases

Stored as

TableHolds
ArrearDetailEmployeeId, OriginYear, OriginMonth, SalaryComponentId, OldAmount, NewAmount, Difference, PaidInPayrollRunId, Status — one row per component per origin month.
SalaryComponent ARREARAn earning with CalculationType = Arrear — the payslip line the totals roll into (broken down by origin month).
PayrollLineThe June line carries the combined gross + arrear statutory (no recomputation downstream).

Edge cases handled

  • Ceiling crossing — if an origin month's PF wage was already at ₹15,000, the arrear adds no PF (per-period rule catches this automatically).
  • ESI coverage — arrears for a month the employee was ESI-covered attract ESI even if they've since crossed ₹21,000; the origin-month rule decides.
  • Negative arrears — a retrospective cut produces negative differences → a recovery, bounded by the negative-net guard (never drives net below zero; shortfall carries forward).
  • Mid-period joiners / LOP — recompute uses the actual attendance of each origin month, so LOP months arrear correctly.
  • Already-arreared months — once an ArrearDetail row is stamped PaidInPayrollRunId, it is never re-paid; re-revising recomputes only the still-unpaid delta.
  • Finalized integrity — past finalized runs are never edited; arrears are always paid forward in an open run, keeping history immutable.
